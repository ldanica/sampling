I believe that my sample population is sufficient and can be representative of my target population. My target population is adults aged 18-65. By surveying students at the university, I expect to get a good amount of young people. According to the U of T website, the student population is over 68,400 people. This includes a large population of undergraduate students, as well as a significant amount of graduate students and post-docs providing me with a wide range of ages (approximately 18-30 years old). 

The high proportion of young people could create a bias in my results which is why I am also recruiting faculty and employees. Such a large institution would have a large population of non-students. By surveying this group of people, I should again be able to obtain results from a lot of people within a wide range of age groups.

Finally, you could argue that sampling only the university is not reflective of the whole adult population. Perhaps the students all have shared experiences at the school that could effect the results of the questionnaire. I believe this issue is not significant enough to impact our results. Going to the same university doesn't mean everyone has the same musical taste. This problem is also offset by sampling non-students. 

By recruiting people at the university, we will have a large sample size to work with while keeping costs down because we have easy access to the population.

3. Could the purpose of your survey have been fulfilled through an observational study? If yes, how? If no, why not?

    ```
    write your answer here...
    ```
I think this survey could be partially fulfilled through an observational study. If we were to have access to data from Apple, Spotify, Youtube, and Amazon Music we could find a lot of the information we are looking for. For example, we would be able to know how many people listened to the most current songs and albums. From the user accounts, we could determine the age of those people. We could then compare it to the age of users that listen to music from 5 years ago, 10 years ago etc. 

Realistically, we probably won't be given access to this data. It also excludes people who don't use these specific apps or don't listen to music through streaming. The data from the user accounts could also be inaccurate. People could be lying about their age. Multiple people could be using the same account and so it's not a true representation of a person but of multiple people that could be of different ages with different musical taste.

Finally, I think for accuracy we cannot rely only only on data from streaming apps. It could be excluding older people who don't use streaming apps causing an undercoverage of these people in our sample population.
